# Qwen3.5-9B survives Hermes's 64K gate

Across thirteen open-weight model families and roughly forty individual checkpoints, only one model clears all three of Hermes's hard constraints — genuine ≥64K native context, a comfortable fit in 16GB of RTX A4000 VRAM, and a current, cleanly-sourced tool-calling benchmark — without a major asterisk: **Qwen3.5-9B**. Its native context is **262,144 tokens**, reached through continued pretraining rather than a runtime RoPE trick, its Q4_K_M weights run **6.17GB**, and its hybrid linear/full-attention architecture keeps the KV cache for 64K tokens to an estimated **~2.15GB**, leaving the most VRAM headroom of any surviving candidate. On tool-calling it posts **0.661 on BFCL v4** — the highest current, cleanly-sourced score found among every model that also clears the context and VRAM gates in this research. The catch, and it is a real one given today's Qwen3-14B lesson, is that Qwen3.5-9B's 262K context figure is corroborated only by two independent secondary analyses rather than a directly-quoted primary technical report, and whether llama.cpp's implementation actually gives its linear-attention layers the small, constant-size KV footprint the architecture promises — rather than silently falling back to a full dense cache — has not been empirically verified. A more conservative fallback, DeepSeek-R1-0528-Qwen3-8B, trades that BFCL-v4 lead for a fully self-documented, internally-consistent context claim and the most VRAM slack of any confirmed candidate, at the cost of weaker and more scattered tool-calling evidence plus a known function-calling integration complaint. Every other family — GLM, MiniCPM, InternLM, Yi, Mistral, Llama, Gemma, Phi, Granite, Command-R, and the dedicated function-calling specialists — produced at least one model that nominally clears 64K, but each carries either a VRAM disqualification, an unresolved context claim, or a current tool-calling blind spot that keeps it out of the top spot.

## The Qwen3-14B trap is not a one-off — it recurs in nine of thirteen families

The single clearest pattern across all four research passes is that the exact failure mode that disqualified Qwen3-14B — a large `max_position_embeddings` value sitting on top of a much smaller genuinely-trained context, with no documented extension mechanism — shows up again and again once you look past the headline number. Every stock Qwen3 dense and MoE model from 0.6B to 235B-A22B reports `max_position_embeddings: 40960` with `rope_scaling: null`, which decomposes to the real trained 32,768 plus an 8,192 output-reservation buffer — the identical shape of the original mistake. InternLM's entire lineup fails the same way but more transparently: `internlm3-8b-instruct`'s own config lists `max_position_embeddings: 32768` while relying on a 6x DynamicNTK runtime scaling factor to advertise a million-token window, and the InternLM2.5-1M config explicitly pairs a 32K-trained base with `rope_scaling: {"type": "dynamic", "factor": 2.5}` — DynamicNTK is an inference-time interpolation trick, not continued pretraining ([InternLM2 Technical Report](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.17297)). MiniCPM3-4B and MiniCPM4-8B show the trap in its cleanest form: their `rope_scaling.original_max_position_embeddings` field is set equal to `max_position_embeddings` (both 32,768), meaning the "long context" scaling object is present but inert — reaching MiniCPM4's advertised 128K requires a user to manually edit `long_factor` arrays into the config themselves, a step OpenBMB documents but does not ship enabled ([MiniCPM4 paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.07900)). The two DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen checkpoints (14B/32B) produced the most genuinely unresolved case in the entire research set: their configs claim 131,072 with `rope_scaling: null`, but a direct read of the DeepSeek-R1 paper (arXiv 2501.12948) shows the commonly-cited "32,768 was the distillation training context" claim is actually a benchmark-evaluation output-length cap, not a training-context statement — leaving no documented mechanism for either the 131,072 claim or the popular 32,768 counter-claim, an ambiguity serious enough that this research explicitly declines to place those two models on any shortlist pending an empirical needle-in-a-haystack test.

Against that backdrop, the models that do pass the gate split into two credibility tiers. The high-confidence tier has an explicit, named continued-pretraining process described in a primary paper or technical report: Yi-6B-200K and Yi-34B-200K ("the context length was extended to 200K through lightweight continual pretraining... continued on about 5B [tokens of] length-upsampled data," with needle-in-a-haystack accuracy rising from 89.3% to 99.8% — [Yi technical report](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.04652)), GLM-4-9B-Chat and GLM-4-9B-Chat-1M (a documented "two-stage pre-training strategy, first expanding the context length to 128K, then to 1M" — [GLM-4 technical report](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.12793)), Llama 3.1/3.2/3.3 and their derivatives (Meta's own `llama3`-type RoPE calibration, baked into the checkpoint with `original_max_position_embeddings: 8192` and a documented continued-pretraining stage), Gemma 3 4B/12B/27B (Google's Gemma 3 Technical Report explicitly validates a 32K→128K extension via RoPE base-frequency changes plus linear scaling, with long-context evals included), Phi-4-mini's LongRoPE (calibrated factor arrays shipped directly in the checkpoint config, not a runtime guess), and IBM's Granite 3.x and 4.0-H families (a documented progressive long-context training stage, with the 4.0-H hybrid Mamba2/attention models going further still — most of their layers aren't RoPE-based at all, so a `rope_scaling: null` field isn't even a relevant red flag there). The lower-confidence-but-still-passing tier covers models whose context claim rests on secondary analysis rather than a directly-quoted primary source: Qwen3.5-9B's 262,144 native context is attested by two independent deep-dive write-ups rather than Qwen's own technical report PDF (which search could not retrieve), and Qwen2.5-14B/7B-Instruct-1M's 256K figure, while sourced to Qwen's own blog, uses the model's Dual Chunk Attention extrapolation technique for anything *beyond* 256K — irrelevant here since 64K sits well inside the trained range regardless.

## VRAM math on a 16GB A4000 eliminates nearly everything above 9B

Once native context is settled, the 16GB ceiling does most of the remaining elimination work, and it does so almost entirely on total parameter count rather than active parameters — a distinction that sinks several MoE models that look efficient on paper. Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B passes the context gate at 262,144 tokens but its Q4_K_M GGUF alone is **48.73GB**, nowhere close to fitting regardless of how cheap its linear-attention layers are per token. The same logic eliminates Qwen3.5/3.6-27B (17.98GB weights alone) and Qwen3.5/3.6-35B-A3B (22.29GB) even before KV cache is added, and it eliminates GLM-4.5-Air (106B total/12B active, ~61-73GB at Q4_K_M) and every Command-R variant above 7B (35B, 104B, 111B) on the same principle — a MoE's "active parameters" number describes compute cost per token, not the VRAM required to hold the model resident. On the dense side, Mistral Small 3.1/3.2 24B and Magistral Small fail for a more mundane reason: their 14.33GB Q4_K_M weights plus even aggressively quantized KV cache push the total to roughly 16.8GB, just over budget, while Gemma 3 27B's 16.55GB of weights alone consumes nearly the entire budget before any KV cache is counted.

Among the survivors, architecture shapes the margin more than parameter count does. Qwen3.5-9B's hybrid design — Gated-DeltaNet linear-attention layers interleaved with full-attention layers at roughly a 3:1 ratio — means only the full-attention minority carries a KV cache that grows with context length, producing an estimated **~2.15GB KV footprint at 64K tokens** against dense-model estimates of 8-10GB for similarly-sized Llama/Mistral checkpoints with the same context length. Gemma 3's hybrid local/global attention shows the same effect for a different reason: five sliding-window layers (capped at a 1,024-token window regardless of total context) for every one global layer makes its KV cache roughly **6x cheaper** than a dense model with equivalent GQA head counts, which is why Gemma 3 12B fits in an estimated 11.6GB total despite having more parameters than several eliminated competitors. Granite-4.0-H-Tiny goes furthest in this direction: its Mamba2 layers hold a small, fixed-size recurrent state instead of a per-token-growing cache at all, and IBM's own claim of "over 70% reduction in memory usage... in long-context workloads" is architecturally credible given how few of its 40 layers are full-attention. The two tightest fits among confirmed passers are Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct-1M, which only clears 16GB at 64K if the KV cache is quantized to Q8 or Q4 (the default fp16 KV cache overflows to 21.9GB), and Mistral Nemo 12B, which overflows by roughly 1.5GB at fp16 KV and needs the same `--cache-type-k/v q8_0` flag to fit.

## Qwen3.5-9B's current BFCL-v4 score separates it from every other survivor

Tool-calling evidence is where this research found the widest confidence gap between models that otherwise look similarly qualified on context and VRAM. Hermes needs a model with strong, reliable function-calling for agentic coding and ops work, and the task explicitly asked to weight current BFCL v4-era numbers over stale pre-2026 figures — which immediately rules out treating several plausible-looking candidates as equivalent, because a BFCL v4 number for the exact surviving checkpoint simply does not exist for most of them. Qwen3.5-9B scores **0.661 on BFCL v4** (2026-07-18 snapshot), the clearest current, model-specific figure found anywhere in this research among candidates that also pass the context and VRAM gates ([BFCL-v4 data via benchlm.ai/llm-stats.com](https://llm-stats.com/benchmarks/bfcl-v4)). Nothing else comes close on a clean sourcing basis: Gemma 3 12B-it posts 30.43% but only in **Prompt mode** (it has no native function-calling chat template, requiring grammar-constrained decoding rather than a trained-in tool-call format — a real integration cost for an agent framework); Mistral Nemo 12B posts 27.63% in native FC mode; Llama 3.1 8B-Instruct has only a Prompt-mode figure of 25.83% with no FC-mode score located at all ([benchmarklist.com BFCL-v4 table](https://benchmarklist.com/benchmarks/bfcl_v4/)). IBM's Granite 4.2 8B scores 52.39% on BFCL v4 ([benchlm.ai](https://benchlm.ai/benchmarks/bfcl-v4)), which is a meaningfully strong number — but it belongs to a sibling model in the same generation, not to Granite-4.0-H-Tiny itself, leaving that candidate's own tool-calling quality an open question rather than a measured fact.

Several otherwise-attractive candidates turn out to have no current tool-calling evidence at all. Phi-4-mini-instruct, Command-R7B, GLM-4-9B-Chat/Chat-1M, Yi-6B-200K, Hermes-3-Llama-3.1-8B, and Functionary-small-v3.2 all cleared the context and (mostly) VRAM gates but surfaced zero BFCL v4 scores in this research — for GLM specifically, the only number found (76.7% on BFCL v3) belongs to the full GLM-4.5 flagship, not the small checkpoint under consideration, and attributing it to GLM-4-9B would repeat the exact kind of unverified-leap error this whole gate exists to catch. The dedicated function-calling specialists fare only slightly better: Llama-xLAM-2-8b-fc-r posts a strong 69-70% multi-turn accuracy, but only on **BFCL v3**, with no v4 figure located, and DeepSeek-R1-0528-Qwen3-8B — which otherwise has the cleanest, most VRAM-comfortable context story of any candidate via a self-documented YaRN extension — shows a weaker BFCL_v3_MultiTurn score of 37.0 plus an open GitHub issue describing real friction getting its function-calling template to work correctly ([vLLM issue #19001](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/19001)). That issue is specific to vLLM rather than llama.cpp, so it may not transfer directly to Hermes's actual serving stack, but it is a documented data point against the model's tool-calling reliability regardless of engine.

## Qwen3.5-9B's newest-architecture risk is real but scoped and recently fixed

The honest cost of picking Qwen3.5-9B is architecture-maturity risk, not a context-length lie. Its hybrid attention implementation in llama.cpp had a confirmed server crash under parallel/batched request handling — reported as "Chunk not found" when request slots were reused under load ([ggml-org/llama.cpp#20222](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/20222)) — which has since been fixed via PR #20232 and verified under production load by the original reporter. The bug only affected multi-slot parallel serving; single-request inference (`--parallel 1`) was stable throughout, which matters directly for Hermes since the project has already capped `max_concurrent_sessions` at 3 after an earlier single-slot crash, meaning Hermes was never going to run Qwen3.5-9B at high parallel concurrency in the first place. The more consequential open question is whether llama.cpp's Qwen3.5 code path actually implements the small, constant-size KV cache the hybrid architecture promises for its linear-attention layers, or silently allocates a full dense cache for all layers by default — this was not independently verified in this research and materially affects whether the ~2.15GB KV-cache estimate underlying Qwen3.5-9B's VRAM headroom is real. Before deploying, running `llama-server --verbose` and reading the actual reported memory allocation at a 64K context would close this gap cheaply and should be treated as a precondition, not an optional nice-to-have, given how directly it bears on whether the model fits as comfortably as estimated.

If that verification turns up a problem, or if Mark wants to avoid betting on the newest architecture in the lineup, DeepSeek-R1-0528-Qwen3-8B is the strongest fallback: its context claim is the most internally consistent of any candidate in this research (`original_max_position_embeddings: 32768` in its own config matches Qwen3-8B's independently-confirmed real native length exactly, with a straightforward 4x YaRN factor reaching 131,072 — no unexplained jump, no inert scaling object), it runs on llama.cpp's long-mature plain-Qwen3 code path with no model-specific issues found, and at Q4_K_M (5.03GB) it fits 16GB comfortably even with an uncompressed fp16 KV cache (≈14GB total), leaving more margin for error than Qwen3.5-9B's smaller but less-proven footprint. Its tool-calling signal is weaker and more scattered than Qwen3.5-9B's, and the vLLM function-calling friction report, while not llama.cpp-specific, is worth testing directly against Hermes's actual chat-template handling before committing. Granite-4.0-H-Tiny is a second-tier fallback worth keeping in view: its context story is arguably the most architecturally robust of all (Mamba2 hybrid layers aren't RoPE-dependent, so there's no scaling mechanism to second-guess at all), and its VRAM footprint is the smallest of any confirmed candidate, but llama.cpp's hybrid Mamba2/attention support is newer (merged via PR #13550 in roughly May 2025) and the model's own BFCL score — as opposed to a same-generation sibling's — remains unmeasured.

## Conclusion

The most important finding of this research is not which single model wins, but how systemic the Qwen3-14B failure pattern turned out to be: nine of the thirteen families surveyed had at least one flagship-looking checkpoint whose advertised context length was a `max_position_embeddings`-plus-runtime-scaling illusion rather than a genuinely trained window, and two more (the DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen 14B/32B checkpoints) produced a contradiction serious enough that neither the vendor's claim nor the commonly-repeated correction could be verified from primary sources. That makes the context-verification step, not the benchmark step, the real bottleneck in choosing a local tool-calling model — a lesson Hermes's rejection logic is already enforcing correctly. Qwen3.5-9B earns the top recommendation by being the only model that combines a plausible (if not fully primary-sourced) pass on that hard gate with the best current, cleanly-attributed tool-calling number found anywhere in the survey and genuine architectural VRAM headroom; its risk is concentrated in one checkbox — verifying llama.cpp's actual KV-cache behavior for its hybrid layers — rather than spread across unresolved context or benchmark uncertainty the way nearly every alternative's risk is. Running that one verification step before deployment, and keeping DeepSeek-R1-0528-Qwen3-8B staged as a same-architecture-family fallback if it fails, is the fastest path to a decision Mark can actually trust more than today's Qwen3-14B pick turned out to deserve.
